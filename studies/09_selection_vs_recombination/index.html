1. Biological Rationale and Historical Context

A perennial challenge in molecular evolution is the confounding between positive Darwinian diversifying selection and homologous recombination. Episodic bursts of positive selection (omega = dN/dS > 1.0) and severe among-site rate heterogeneity (alpha < 0.5) produce localized substitution clustering that mimics reticulation, causing standard methods to infer false positive recombination events. Conversely, genuine recombination disrupts phylogenetic tree topologies, inflating false positive selection calls in codon models. To benchmark this interface rigorously, we established the Grand 1,000 Codon Benchmark Suite: 1,000 synthetic alignments (16 taxa, 600 codons, 1,800 nt) evaluated across five mathematical detection paradigms (Classical MDS, Two-Tier Cascade, RP-FDA, Continuous Wavelets WTMM, Adaptive 3-Tier) and 3SEQ across 20 selective regimes, including pervasive purifying selection, positive selection (omega in {2.0, 5.0}), and heavy-tailed Gamma rate variation (alpha in [0.1, 10.0]).

2. Systematic Benchmark Evaluation and Key Findings

The benchmark established distinct operational boundaries across algorithmic paradigms. Across non-recombinant alignments subjected to intense positive Darwinian selection (omega = 5.0), both RP-FDA and the Two-Tier Cascade maintained a 0.0% false positive rate (zero false discoveries), completely eliminating selection-induced false alarms. In contrast, unpolished scalar changepoint metrics suffered up to 8.0% false positive rates under severe rate clustering. On recombinant alignments, the Two-Tier Cascade achieved 87.0% to 100.0% detection sensitivity with 69.0% exact taxon parentage attribution, executing in 79.7 milliseconds per alignment. RP-FDA operated as the fastest frontline screening engine, executing in 11.5 milliseconds (9.5x faster than 3SEQ at 109.1 ms) with 0.0% false positives.

Classical multidimensional scaling (MDS) tracks scalar distance shifts but lacks directional parentage resolution. Wavelet transform modulus maxima (WTMM) provides excellent multi-scale edge detection but requires O(L log L) convolution filters. Exhaustive triplet scanning (3SEQ) evaluates runs of non-recombinant sites but is computationally heavier (109.1 ms) and lacks codon-aware rate calibration. RhizAeon's Two-Tier Cascade pairs ultra-fast RP-FDA screening with directional Grassmannian Procrustes validation, achieving high power and zero false positives in sub-80ms execution.
