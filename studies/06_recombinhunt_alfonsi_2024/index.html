1. Biological Rationale and Historical Context

In 2024, Alfonsi and colleagues published RecombinHunt in Nature Communications, presenting a large-scale synthetic benchmark to evaluate viral recombination detection under realistic sequencing noise. They generated 10,500 full-length 29,903-nucleotide SARS-CoV-2 genomes archived in Supplementary Data 1 of their publication. The benchmark spans 21 parameter cells of 500 independent genomes each: seven cells represent clonal negative controls (0-BP BA.2 nulls) carrying 1 to 30 private noise mutations; seven cells represent single-crossover mosaics (1-BP BA.2/AY.45 recombinants) with 0 to 30 noise mutations; and seven cells represent double-crossover cassette insertions (2-BP BA.2/AY.45/BA.2 mosaics) under identical noise tiers.

2. Systematic Benchmark Evaluation and Key Findings

RhizAeon processed the complete 10,500-genome compendium in 21.2 seconds total wall-clock time on a single CPU core, averaging 2.02 milliseconds per 30-kb genome (throughput: 495 genomes/second). Across all 3,500 non-recombinant null genomes, RhizAeon achieved a flawless 0.00% false positive rate (zero false discoveries across 3,500 replicates). In contrast, RecombinHunt's empirical false positive rate escalated steadily with noise, reaching 4.0% at 15 mutations and 8.8% at 30 mutations. On single-crossover mosaics, RhizAeon maintained 100.0% sensitivity across every noise tier, placing 100.0% of inferred breakpoints within the true flanking SNP interval (where RecombinHunt sensitivity fell to 92.8%). On double-crossover cassettes, RhizAeon sustained 99.8% sensitivity and 94.2% exact interval accuracy even under 30 noise substitutions, while RecombinHunt sensitivity dropped to 78.8%.

RecombinHunt relies on dictionary matching of lineage-defining mutations against precomputed reference tables. While faster than phylogenetic heuristics, dictionary matching becomes vulnerable when convergent private mutations accumulate, leading to false-positive mosaic calls in hypermutated strains. Traditional phylogenetic methods like GARD scale at >300 seconds per genome, rendering 10,000-genome cohorts completely intractable. RhizAeon operates directly on coordinate distance tensors without precomputed dictionary matrices or guide trees, executing in 2.02 ms with a memory footprint under 120 megabytes.
